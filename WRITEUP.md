# Slot Floor Review: Do Not Replace the Bottom 10

**Recommendation: do not replace the ten lowest machines. Inspect one, run two promotion weekends, and put up signs in Back Forty. Spend $38,700 of the $100,000 and keep $61,300.**

**The ten lowest machines.** Only M-1158 has a machine-specific problem: it pays on 38.5% of spins against 31.5% published (z ≈ 124), so its hold is 3.0% against a 10% design. That is a lead to inspect, not proof of wrongdoing. The other nine are low for ordinary reasons: Back Forty earns about two-thirds of a typical zone and runs low-margin games, and three were not on the floor all period. Luck cost the ten about $63k.

**Demand and downtime.** Saturdays run about 60% above Tuesdays. Super Bowl +23%, March Madness +19%, our Slot Tournament +18%, EDC +16%, conventions only +2–3%. The Back Forty walkway closure cut that zone's share by 31%, then it recovered. Downtime cost $90k of expected win (0.38%); Ridgeline breaks down about four times as often as Halcyon.

**Guests and comps.** Card holders wagered $163.8M and lost $15.6M, in line with expected loss. The top 1% account for 20% of losses. Around April 28 comps became about twice as cheap, raising comp cost per $1,000 wagered by 10%.

**Models** (trained Jan–Apr, tested May–Jun).
1. *Floor forecast*, weekday pattern × recent level: $64.9k a day error (4.9%) against $218k for the average day. It sets the July baseline.
2. *Machine model*, gradient-boosted trees: $950 per machine-day against $1,411 for the average. A zone × game × weekday table is about as accurate ($936), so we use it to estimate what a slot earns with a different game. Even the best swap gains $1.1–2.8k per machine over 16 days against a $3,380 lease.
